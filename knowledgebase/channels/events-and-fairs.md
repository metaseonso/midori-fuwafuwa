# ⏰ Events and fairs

> ⚠️ **Time-sensitive.** All dates, fees and application windows verified **August 2026**. Fair
> calendars shift annually and fees rise. **Re-verify every date on the official site before
> spending anything.**

## 🔴 The live deadline — Bologna, closes 26 October 2026

**The Bologna Children's Book Fair Illustrators Exhibition** is the most credentialing prize in
children's illustration, and the 2027 call is **open now**.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Window** | 28 July – **26 October 2026** |
| **Fee** | €25 + VAT (professionals) |
| **Submit** | Exactly **5 illustrations on one theme.** Unpublished, or published after 1 Jan 2025 |
| **Format** | JPG/PNG, max 5MB, 150 DPI |
| **Eligibility** | Born before 31 Dec 2009 |
| **Selection rate** | **~1.7%** (75 selected from 4,158 illustrators in 2026) |
| **Finalist rate** | **~7.4%** (~317 shortlisted) |

**What finalists get:** free BCBF 2027 admission, a slot at the **Portfolio Marathon** (a full day
of art-director portfolio reviews reserved for finalists), publication consideration.

**What winners get:** the **Illustrators Annual**, distributed in Italian, English, **Japanese,
Korean and Chinese** — plus a touring exhibition that goes to **Japan, Korea and China.** Winners
under 35 are auto-shortlisted for the **International Award for Illustration (€15,000).**

**Why it dominates everything else here:** €25, no travel, and a genuinely non-trivial hit rate on
the single most valuable credential in the field. Every other opportunity in this note is a
several-thousand-dollar bet.

### 🚫 But: Bologna forbids AI tools outright

The rules state **"no artificial intelligence tools permitted"** in creation. **MoCCA** goes
further and rules AI-generated *or AI-assisted* work ineligible.

The studio's settled position is that [[../legal/ai-made-art-and-copyright|AI-made art is fine]].
That position is unchanged — but it **closes these two specific doors.**

This is a real fork, and it should be chosen rather than drifted into:
- **Keep a hand-drawn track** for these specific opportunities, or
- **Accept the doors are closed** and put the effort into channels that do not care.

Both are legitimate. Only sleepwalking into it is not.

## The comics-first gate — and the cheap unlock

**Five of six live events are comics-first, not illustration-first.** They want printed,
sequential, for-sale material:

- **MoCCA** requires "some published material" (self-published accepted)
- **TCAF** explicitly prioritises "exhibitors who have comics in print for sale"
- **Short Run** is a comix festival
- **Thought Bubble** is a comic convention

A picture-book dummy and concept art is a weak application everywhere.

> **The unlock is one cheap artifact: a zine or mini-comic of the characters.**
>
> One print run makes the studio eligible at **MoCCA, TCAF, Thought Bubble and Short Run
> simultaneously** — and gives an audience something to physically walk away holding, which serves
> the actual goal better than any table does.

**This should probably precede any travel spending at all.**

### Correction worth knowing
**TCAF is free to *attend*, not free to *exhibit*.** $514 CAD for a 6ft table. A commonly repeated
error.

## Geography — the founder is US-based

⚠️ The source research assumed a Korea-based studio (inferred from the Korean visual language) and
concluded that no event justifies a dedicated trip. **That conclusion does not apply here.**

The founder is US-based, which means:
- **MoCCA (NYC) and Short Run (Seattle) are domestic.** Table cost is the real cost.
- **TCAF (Toronto)** is a short hop.
- **Bologna and Thought Bubble** are the genuinely international ones — and Bologna is the only one
  worth an intercontinental trip, mainly because being selected makes the trip near-free.

**Fees are trivial next to travel.** £115–$514 for a table versus flights, several nights, and
freight. Geography is the cost driver, not the fee.
