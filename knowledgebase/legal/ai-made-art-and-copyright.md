# AI-made art and copyright

**Founders' position: AI-made art is fine. It does not have to be human-made.** Settled
2026-08-19.

On examination, the copyright concern raised earlier was framed too narrowly. Here is the accurate
version.

## What is actually true

**A single AI-generated image, taken alone, is not independently protectable by copyright.** The
US Copyright Office position is that copyright protects human authorship, and prompting alone —
however detailed or iterated — does not establish it, because the system determines the specific
expressive output. *Thaler v. Perlmutter* settled the far edge: fully autonomous AI output with no
human author is not copyrightable.

That much is real.

## Why it barely touches this studio

**1. A book is a compilation, and the compilation is human-authored.**
The story, character names, dialogue, page sequence, layout, and the choice of which image goes
where — all human, all protected. That is what a book *is*. Anyone copying the book copies all of it.

**2. The text alone is fully protected.**
For a children's book series, the words are a first-class work in their own right.

**3. A character is not an image.**
Characters get protection through their specific expression across a body of work, and — where used
commercially — through [[trademark-is-territorial|trademark]] on names and marks. Neither depends
on any single illustration's authorship.

**4. Selection and arrangement is protectable even when the parts are AI-generated.**
This is established: in the graphic-novel case the Copyright Office examined, the creator's
arrangement of panels and her own text were protected, while the raw AI-generated panels were not.
The *made thing* was hers.

**5. Trademark does the real commercial work anyway.**
For a studio building an audience and possibly merchandise, trademark on the studio name, logo, and
character names is what actually stops a competitor. Per-illustration copyright registration is not
how small studios defend brands — with or without AI.

**6. The realistic threat model is wholesale copying or brand impersonation.**
Both are actionable on the human-authored layers: text, arrangement, and trademark.

## Where it genuinely does matter

Worth knowing. Not worth slowing down for.

- **Publishers and licensees run IP diligence.** Some have AI disclosure requirements or policies.
  If the series goes to a publisher, or a character gets licensed to a third party, expect the
  question and answer it plainly.
- **Some distribution platforms require AI disclosure** — Amazon KDP among them. Procedural, not a
  barrier.

## Practical takeaway

**Put effort into the writing and into trademark. Those are what protect this.** Do not slow art
production over image-level copyright.
