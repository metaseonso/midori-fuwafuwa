# ⚠️ China is first-to-file

**Founder's assessment, recorded verbatim: "that china bit is really annoying and stupid."**

Agreed. It is genuinely a bad system, and the annoyance is the correct reaction. It is also real,
and ignoring it is the single most common way small studios lose their own brand name abroad.

## The problem

In most countries, trademark rights connect to **use** — you built the brand, so you have a claim,
and registration mostly formalises what you already earned.

**China is first-to-file.** Whoever registers a mark first owns it. **Full stop.**

- It does not matter that you used it first.
- It does not matter that you created it.
- It does not matter that you are obviously the originator.
- Your years of building the brand elsewhere count for very little.

## Why this hits studios specifically

Trademark squatting in China is not opportunistic — it is **an organised industry**. Squatters
monitor foreign brands gaining traction internationally, register the names pre-emptively, and
then either:

1. Charge you a large sum to transfer the mark you created, or
2. Block your goods at customs, or
3. Manufacture and sell using *your* brand, legally, because they own it there.

Point 3 is the one that ruins studios. And because most physical merchandise in the world is
manufactured in China, a squatted mark can block you from **making your own merch** — even if you
never intended to sell a single unit inside China.

## What to actually do

**Register in China early — before you need it, not when you need it.**

Specifically:
- If merchandise is **anywhere** in the plan, register in China. Manufacturing alone is the reason.
- If Chinese sales are plausible, register in China.
- Register both the **Latin name** ("Midori Fuwafuwa") **and a Chinese-character version.** If you
  do not choose your own Chinese name, the market will invent one for you and someone else may
  register it.
- Consider registering **defensively across classes**, not just the one class you need today.

This can go through the [[madrid-protocol|Madrid Protocol]] as one designation among several — it
does not require a separate adventure. It just should not be the designation you postpone.

## The honest cost/benefit

| | Cost |
|---|---|
| Registering in China early | A few hundred USD in fees, as part of a Madrid filing you are doing anyway |
| Recovering a squatted mark later | Thousands to tens of thousands, months to years, and often failure |

It is a bad rule, and the cheap move is to comply with it early rather than argue with it late.
