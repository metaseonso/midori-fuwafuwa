# Copyright is already global

**Your artwork and stories are protected in ~180 countries the moment you make them. You did
nothing to get this, and there is nothing to do.**

This is the **Berne Convention**. Member countries agreed to protect each other's works
automatically. No registration, no filing, no fee, no notice, no application. Protection attaches
when the work is *fixed* — drawn, written, saved.

So when the question is "does this hold up globally," for copyright the answer is: **yes, already,
without you doing anything.**

## What this covers

- Illustrations and paintings
- Stories, text, dialogue, character names as used in text
- The arrangement and sequence of a book
- Website copy
- Code, where it contains real creative expression

## The only thing worth adding

A visible notice. Not required — protection exists without it — but it makes an "I didn't know"
defence harder for someone who copies you.

```
© 2026 Midori Fuwafuwa Studio™. All rights reserved.
```

## What registration is, and isn't

US copyright **registration** is a separate, optional, US-specific thing. It does **not** grant
protection — you already have that. What it buys is *enforcement power in US courts*:

- It is a prerequisite to filing an infringement suit over a US work.
- It unlocks statutory damages and attorney's fees, instead of having to prove actual financial loss.
- Filed within 5 years of publication, it creates a legal presumption of ownership.

Useful eventually. Not urgent, and not what makes the work yours.
