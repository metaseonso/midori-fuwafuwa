# Joint authorship and credit

The studio is two people. The founders describe the split as: **his artwork, her inspiration** —
"she owns the muse capacity, I own the genius, as the Greeks say."

The question was how to express that in writing. Here is the shape.

## Three dials, not one

Most people assume these must match. They do not, and separating them is what makes an honest
asymmetric arrangement possible.

| Dial | What it means | Here |
|---|---|---|
| **Authorship** | Who made the fixed expression | He drew it → he is the author |
| **Ownership** | Who holds the rights | Can be both, via a shared entity, regardless of who drew it |
| **Economics** | Who gets paid what | Any split they agree — 50/50 is fine even with sole authorship |

**Inspiration is not authorship in law.** Copyright protects fixed expression, never ideas. This is
not a judgement about creative contribution — it is just what the statute reaches. Her role can be
essential to the work existing and still create no copyright interest.

## Why that is useful rather than awkward

It means the **default joint-authorship risk does not apply here.** Where two people co-author a
work with no agreement, each holds an equal undivided interest and **either can license the work
unilaterally** — the other's only recourse is a share of the money, not a veto.

If he is the sole author of the artwork, that risk is absent by default. The gap to close is the
opposite one: making sure the felt partnership is reflected in ownership and economics, since law
alone would not put it there.

## How to express it

1. **Name the roles honestly rather than pretending symmetry.** Something like Creative Director /
   Author and Muse / Creative Inspiration. Written asymmetry is stronger than a fictional 50/50.
2. **Assign IP into a shared entity.** Both own it through the entity, whoever drew it.
3. **Set economics independently.** The revenue split does not have to track authorship.
4. **Add a credit term.** She is named as muse on the work, in perpetuity. This is a real,
   enforceable contractual promise even though it is not copyright.
5. **Vary per production.** Whimsy is hers in origin — that production can carry a different split
   or name her as a creator. Per-production terms are completely normal.

## What a founders' agreement typically covers

- Who owns what: individual work vs. jointly created work
- Assignment of both founders' rights into the entity, if one exists
- Mutual consent required before licensing or selling rights
- What happens to IP and revenue if one founder leaves
- How authorship is defined for future AI-assisted work — see [[ai-made-art-and-copyright]]
- The revenue split
- A dispute-resolution mechanism
