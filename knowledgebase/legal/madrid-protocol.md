# The Madrid Protocol

**One application, filed through WIPO, designating as many member countries as you want.** ~115
members, covering most of the world. One form, one language, one payment.

This is the mechanism that makes international trademark practical instead of absurd. You still
end up with separate national rights — but you did not file separately in each country.

## You must go through a home office first

There is no direct entry to Madrid. You need an **office of origin** — a national trademark office
that certifies your application before it reaches WIPO.

**Midori Fuwafuwa's office of origin: the USPTO.** Founder is US-based (confirmed 2026-08-19).

You qualify for a country's office through **any one** of:
1. Nationality
2. Domicile — you live there
3. A real commercial establishment there

Any single one is enough.

## Sequence

```
1. File a US application with the USPTO          ← your Madrid "basis"
2. File one Madrid application through WIPO      ← designate your market tier
3. Each designated country examines it separately
4. Rights issue per country; switch ™ → ® per country as each issues
5. Add more countries later without refiling
```

## "Sell globally" ≠ "register globally"

This distinction matters and saves a great deal of money.

- **Selling worldwide requires no registration at all.** A website plus print-on-demand plus app
  stores already reaches everyone. Nothing stops you.
- **Registering worldwide is a trap.** Each designated country charges its own fee. All ~115 would
  cost tens of thousands to protect markets where you will never make a sale.

## The market tier to designate

For a studio doing children's books, apps, and possibly merchandise:

| Designation | Why |
|---|---|
| **European Union (EUIPO)** | One designation covers all 27 member states. Best value on the list by a wide margin. |
| **United Kingdom** | Left the EU mark, so it needs its own designation now. |
| **Japan** | Large market for illustrated IP and children's media. |
| **South Korea** | Directly relevant given the studio's Korean visual language. |
| **China** | ⚠️ See [[china-first-to-file]] — the most time-sensitive item here. |
| **Canada** | Cheap addition, adjacent market. |
| **Australia** | Cheap addition, English-language market. |

Seven designations plus the US home registration. That covers the overwhelming majority of
realistic commercial reach.

**Order-of-magnitude cost:** a few thousand USD in official fees for a tier like this, plus
attorney fees if used. Fees vary by country and class count and change over time — check WIPO's
fee calculator before budgeting.
