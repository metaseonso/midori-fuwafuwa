# The Kits

## What it is
An original IP. Planned to start as a **children's book series**.

## Premise
The story of Seonso and Grumpy Carrot's children, on their journey exploring and learning
about the many worlds around us.

## The name
"Kits" carries both meanings at once — fox kits, and kids. The wordplay is load-bearing:
it ties the family's fox nature to the children at the centre of the story.

- **Grumpy Carrot (bunny)** is the *artist persona*.
- **The fox mother** is a *character that artist creates*.

The same holds for Seonso and the fox father. The resemblance between persona and created
character is deliberate, and has an in-world reason the founders are holding for later.

This is a studio-wide structure, not a Kits-only detail — see the main `PROJECT.md`.

## How it fits the studio

**The Kits is Production 01 — the first production, and the reason the website exists.**

The studio's stated website goal is *building an audience for our original IP*. The Kits is
that IP. That makes it the first and primary subject of the site, not one entry among many.

Two direct consequences from the website research (`research/`):

1. **The Kits gets the first Field.** Each production is its own open, explorable diorama
   space. The Kits' "many worlds" premise maps onto this unusually well — the books' worlds
   and the site's Fields are the same idea in two media. Worth designing them together rather
   than adapting one to the other after the fact.
2. **The Kits gets the first Forest** once its repo carries real activity — monthly saplings
   from actual book-production commits.

## Protection — corrected framing, 2026-08-19

**Founders' position: AI-made art is fine. It does not have to be human-made.** Accepted, and
on examination the copyright concern raised earlier was framed too narrowly. Restating it
accurately:

**What is true:** an individual AI-generated image, taken alone, is not independently
protectable by copyright.

**Why that barely touches this production:**

1. **A book is a compilation, and the compilation is human-authored.** The story, the
   character names, the dialogue, the page sequence, the layout, the selection of which image
   goes where — all human. All protectable. That is what a book *is*, and copying the book
   copies all of it.
2. **The text alone is fully protected.** For a children's book series the words are a
   first-class work in their own right.
3. **A character is not an image.** Characters get protection through their specific expression
   across a body of work, and — where used commercially — through **trademark** on names and
   marks. Neither depends on any single illustration's authorship.
4. **Trademark does the real commercial work anyway.** For a studio building an audience and
   possibly merchandise later, trademark on the studio name, the logo, and character names is
   what actually stops a competitor. Per-illustration copyright registration is not how small
   studios defend a brand, whether the art is AI-made or not.
5. **The realistic threat model is wholesale copying or brand impersonation**, and both are
   actionable on the human-authored layers — text, arrangement, and trademark.

**The one place it genuinely matters** — worth knowing, not worth worrying about now:
- **Traditional publishers and licensees run IP diligence.** Some have AI disclosure
  requirements or policies. If the series ever goes to a publisher or a character gets licensed
  to a third party, expect the question and be ready to answer it plainly.
- **Some distribution platforms require AI disclosure** (Amazon KDP, for one). Procedural, not
  a barrier.

**Practical takeaway:** put effort into trademark and into the writing. Those are what protect
this. Do not slow down art production over image-level copyright.
